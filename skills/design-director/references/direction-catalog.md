# Adaptive Direction Catalog

Use this only after deriving a product thesis and first-pass direction from evidence. It widens the option space when ideas converge on generic model defaults; it is not a menu of ready-made themes.

## Derive before naming

Extract:

1. **Task character** — frequent or occasional, fast or contemplative, novice or expert, low or high consequence.
2. **Content character** — textual or visual, sparse or dense, emotional or factual, static or live.
3. **Domain material** — real workflows, artifacts, vocabulary, data shapes, physical materials, history, or culture associated with the product.
4. **Brand character** — restrained or expressive, familiar or novel, institutional or personal, durable or timely.
5. **Platform character** — browser narrative, repeated web workflow, native desktop tool, touch-first, keyboard-first, or mixed input.
6. **Evidence strength** — confirmed brand/user requirements versus assumptions that still need validation.

Choose a useful tension such as “precise but humane,” “dense but calm,” or “playful but trustworthy.” A productive tension prevents one-note styling and makes tradeoffs explicit.

## Style fingerprint

Define concrete choices rather than a trend label:

| Axis | Decision |
|---|---|
| Concept | Product-specific idea, tension, or metaphor |
| Typography | Productive/expressive balance, families, contrast, hierarchy, numeral/script needs |
| Palette | Temperature, saturation, semantic roles, and where contrast peaks |
| Density | Compact, balanced, or spacious; where and why density changes |
| Composition | Symmetric/asymmetric, modular/editorial, fixed/fluid, dominant alignment |
| Geometry | Sharp/soft, radius and border logic, shape vocabulary |
| Material | Flat, ruled, layered, tactile, luminous, image-led |
| Imagery/data | Photography, illustration, diagrams, charts, texture, or none |
| Content voice | Direct, technical, reassuring, editorial, playful, or another product-fit tone |
| Motion | Nearly static, crisp, elastic, cinematic, or system-native |
| Signature | One or two repeatable devices tied to the product |
| Tradeoff | What this direction intentionally does less well |

A signature move might be a product-specific data strip, annotation system, split-pane rhythm, illustration grammar, comparison device, or content transition. A gradient, glow, large heading, or unusual font is not a concept by itself.

Do not expose a catalog family name as the final concept unless it genuinely helps the user. Give each direction a product-specific name and rationale.

## Direction families

### Quiet precision

**Fits:** professional productivity, serious services, focused workflows, trustworthy tools.

- Controlled neutral field and economical accent.
- Typography, alignment, and proportion carry the identity.
- Thin rules or whitespace group content; elevation is rare.
- Motion is brief and functional.

**Failure mode:** anonymous monochrome component-library output. Add a product-specific navigation, type, content, or data motif.

### Editorial intelligence

**Fits:** publishing, research, knowledge tools, portfolios, cultural products, narrative marketing.

- Strong type contrast, annotations, captions, and deliberate reading rhythm.
- Asymmetry or changing column structures support the narrative.
- Images and text share a composition instead of living in interchangeable cards.

**Failure mode:** a poster that makes application tasks slow or text difficult to scan.

### Warm humanism

**Fits:** care, education, community, food, lifestyle, personal services.

- Warm neutrals, natural accents, readable humanist type, candid imagery or illustration.
- Friendliness comes from language, rhythm, and material—not maximum radius.
- Feedback is reassuring without becoming verbose.

**Failure mode:** childish bubbles, low-contrast beige, or friendliness that hides boundaries and errors.

### Industrial utility

**Fits:** developer tools, operations, logistics, manufacturing, infrastructure, expert software.

- Compact rhythm, explicit structure, strong labels, practical monospace accents.
- Rules and panels reflect task architecture rather than decorate it.
- Status color is semantic and economical; keyboard interactions are first class.

**Failure mode:** neon-on-black cyberpunk, tiny text, or noise mistaken for technical depth.

### Analytical instrument

**Fits:** finance, science, monitoring, planning, data-heavy workflows.

- Tables, plots, comparisons, and filters define the architecture.
- Tabular numerals, stable alignment, and precise states support comparison.
- Dense evidence sits beside calmer control and summary areas.
- Color, labels, shape, and ordering communicate data together.

**Failure mode:** decorative KPI tiles, meaningless headline metrics, rainbow charts, or inaccessible hover-only detail.

### Playful modularity

**Fits:** education, creative tools, events, collaborative or youth-focused products.

- Disciplined color, stable modular grid, expressive display type, readable UI type.
- Shape, illustration, and motion teach behavior or reward progress.
- Interaction remains predictable beneath the expression.

**Failure mode:** every color and motion at once, toy-like treatment of serious actions, or inaccessible novelty.

### Graphic boldness

**Fits:** campaigns, entertainment, sports, fashion, launches, creator brands.

- High typographic contrast, decisive blocks, hard crops, and selective grid-breaking.
- A small number of visually dominant moments drive the narrative.
- Mobile composition is designed independently, not scaled down.

**Failure mode:** product workflows turned into posters or content obscured by branding.

### Refined restraint

**Fits:** premium services, hospitality, architecture, high-value commerce, craft.

- Quality comes from proportion, type, imagery, materials, and pacing.
- Nuanced neutrals and precise details replace visible interface noise.
- Confidence permits restraint, but controls remain discoverable.

**Failure mode:** black-and-gold cliché, excessive letter spacing, weak contrast, or emptiness presented as luxury.

### Spatial calm

**Fits:** focus tools, personal organization, wellness, thoughtful consumer software.

- Low visual noise, clear zones, softened but sufficient contrast, progressive disclosure.
- Motion preserves spatial continuity without demanding attention.
- Density increases where repeated work requires it.

**Failure mode:** wasteful empty space, washed-out controls, or essential actions hidden in pursuit of calm.

### Retro-digital character

**Fits:** music, games, media experiments, developer culture, brands with a credible historical link.

- Use a limited set of era cues: bitmap detail, terminal rhythm, print color, early-GUI geometry, or analog texture.
- Modern readable controls and accessibility remain intact.

**Failure mode:** costume design, fake glitches, scanline overload, or nostalgia unrelated to the product.

### Native platform clarity

**Fits:** desktop utilities, file/media tools, system companions, long-session professional software.

- Menus, toolbars, split views, inspectors, tables, dialogs, and shortcuts do real work.
- Brand expression lives in content, iconography, type, and selected surfaces while behavior follows the OS.

**Failure mode:** a responsive website in a window or custom replacements for every native convention.

## Generate genuinely different options

For each Direction-mode option provide:

- a product-specific name, not only a family label;
- a one-sentence experience promise;
- at least five fingerprint decisions, including composition or hierarchy;
- one signature move tied to content or interaction;
- one meaningful tradeoff;
- one responsive or platform consequence;
- why it fits the product thesis.

Reject and regenerate when options differ only by palette, font, theme, radius, illustration, or surface treatment. Contrast composition, density, hierarchy, navigation, and interaction where the product allows it.

When the user delegates the choice, recommend one. Do not make a design-inexperienced user choose between unexplained aesthetic labels.

## Combine carefully

Combine at most two compatible families and give each a clear role:

- analytical instrument for the app shell + editorial intelligence for reports;
- industrial utility for workflow density + warm humanism for guidance;
- native platform clarity for behavior + refined restraint for brand expression.

Do not average incompatible ideas across every element.

## Anti-default diagnostic

Challenge a concept when it relies on several of these without product evidence:

- sidebar plus uniform rounded-card grid;
- giant low-information hero, statistic, or floating product mockup;
- cream-and-serif, dark-and-neon, or purple/cyan glow used as automatic brand identity;
- pills for all controls and labels;
- colored icon tile beside every heading;
- tracked eyebrow text or ornamental monospace metadata repeated throughout;
- arrows appended to nearly every link or call to action;
- invented dashboards, metrics, testimonials, or activity;
- one radius, surface, and shadow treatment everywhere;
- centered section after centered section;
- motion added to every entrance rather than meaningful transitions.

These devices are valid when the concept requires them. The problem is unexamined repetition.

## Stress-test the direction

Before selection, ask:

1. Would it still feel tied to this product with the logo hidden?
2. Does the task hierarchy survive grayscale, long content, empty content, and error states?
3. Can its signature move recompose at narrow widths or small windows without becoming decoration?
4. Does it preserve familiar behavior for common controls?
5. Is its tradeoff acceptable for the primary user and frequency of use?
6. Is the distinctive part feasible within the current stack, assets, budget, and accessibility constraints?

Revise the direction when any answer is materially weak.

## Reference research protocol

Choose a small set of references with comparable constraints:

1. one official platform or accessibility source;
2. one or two live products with a similar task, density, or audience;
3. one reference for the desired brand/editorial character;
4. optionally one counter-reference that demonstrates what to avoid.

For each, record:

- source and relevant screen or pattern;
- comparable constraint;
- principle to borrow;
- element not to copy;
- accessibility or implementation risk.

Do not copy layouts or proprietary brand assets. Inspiration-gallery screenshots without live-product context are weak evidence.

## Multiple products

For unrelated apps, compare fingerprints pairwise. If composition, typography, palette, geometry, and signature move substantially repeat, re-derive one direction unless the shared choice follows from similar user needs.

For a product suite, define:

- **shared layer:** brand voice, core color roles, type families, icon grammar, accessibility baseline;
- **adaptive layer:** density, navigation, composition, visualization, and task-specific patterns;
- **forbidden drift:** behavior or terminology that should remain consistent across the suite.
